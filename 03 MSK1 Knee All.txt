⇅Overlap with the Hip (*See "Hip and Knee Flexibility Tests") REPE
	-Muscles crossing the hip joint provide significant stability and control for the knee REPE
	-Lack of flexibility in soft tissues around the hip may influence knee mechanics and tight knee structures may in turn influence hip movements. REPE
	-Assess the flexibility of structures crossing the hip and/or knee joint when evaluating a knee

Stability vs. Mobility
	-The knee joint is quite stable but much less mobile than most other joints
	-Sometimes it is described as having six degrees of freedom, including movements in the following directions [KRP.0.1] 
		but only two of these are related to physiologic movements
			Flexion/Extension (physiological) 
			Internal/External Rotation (physiological) Γ(screw-home mechanism)
		The rest are more closely related to accessory movements or planes in which only very minimal functional movement occurs
			Varus/Valgus (Slight movement, minimally functional) 
			Anterior/Posterior (Accessory) 
			Medial/Lateral (Accessory) 
			Proximal/Distal (Distraction/Compression) 
	-Therefore, the knee is generally thought to have only "two degrees" of freedom

					Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) ("Veins LQ") [MSK1_DVTScreenKnee]
						A deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is Clotting/Blockage of a distal vein:
							-Clots can dislodge and move through circulatory system to central aspects of the circulatory system (heart & lungs) or even the brain – causing a stroke or even death
							-Clots are more common after surgery – especially to the ...↓ regions
								hip
								knee
								leg/calf
								abdominal
								chest
						Some of the reasons why surgery increases DVT risk include: Step.3_CT.Knee (!!E.Grade & Doc).Knee
							-Tissue debris, protein, and fats may move into veins following surgery
							-Vein walls can become damaged, which may also release substances that promote blood clotting
							-Prolonged bed which is common following surgery results in less blood flow and greater accumulation of these substances in distal vessels
							---------------------------------------------------
							-The gold standard and most evidence-based tool for DVT screening of the lower extremity is the use of the "Well’s Criteria Clinical Prediction Rule". [MSK1_WellCritPredRule]
								-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
								("Well’s Criteria Clinical Prediction Rule.png")
								Clinical Feature + Points Allotted for Criterium being Present
									Active cancer (treatment within 6 months, or palliation)                 1
									Paralysis, paresis, or immobilization of lower extremity                 1
									Bedridden for more than 3c days because of surgery (Within 4 weeks)      1
									Localized tenderness along distribution of deep veins                    1
									Entire leg swollen                                                       1 
									Unilateral calf swelling of greater than 3 cm (below tibial tuberosity) -2
									Unilateral pitting edema                                                 1

								------------------------------------------------------------------------------
								-While the Well’s Criteria are required for the most accurate identification of DVT, general warning signs include:
									-Swelling in one or both legs ("Swelling DVT.png")
									-Pain or tenderness in one or both legs, which may occur only while standing or walking
									-Warmth in the skin of the affected leg
									-Red or discolored skin in the affected leg
									-Visible surface veins

						Other Lifestyle Factors that Contribute to Deep Vein Thrombosis formation include:

				3.Fractures surrounding the knee joint / Screening for Fractures [MSK1_FractScreenKnee] [KRP.-2.2.3 ☢]
					-Screening to determine if radiographs are needed. A final screening consideration for the knee involves using validated clinical prediction rules to rule out fracture and/or determine if referral for a radiograph is recommended
					-Note: These types of prediction rules cannot confidently detect the presence of a fracture, but instead are performed to determine if a fracture may be ruled out – in which case a radiograph should be performed
					---------------------------------------------------------
					Ottawa Knee Rules
						-A fracture in the area of the knee may not be ruled out if ANY of these variables are present in the patient:
							Knee x-ray indications: Ottawa knee rules (acute)
								Age ≥ 55 years
								Isolated patella tenderness
								Tenderness at head of fibula
								Inability to flex knee 90°
								Inability to bear weight (4 steps) immediately after injury and in emergency department
								----------------------------------------------
								-If none of these variables are present, a fracture may be ruled out and no radiograph is needed (Sensitivity of 1.0).  
								-Otherwise, a radiograph should be ordered to determine if the patient has experienced a fracture in the area of the knee.

	Assessment/Quantification of Knee Swelling [MSK1_Imm2B_Knee] [MSK1_Imm2B_Knee_Swell]
		Joint Edema/Joint Effusion
			-The manner in which swelling presents in a knee may provide information about the underlying problem(s)
			-Joint Edema is a general term for swelling
			-Edema may occur both inside and outside of the confines of a joint
				All effusions are edemas, but not all edemas are effusions (investigar més Edemes ¿?)
					Γ All effusions provoke swelling but not all the swellings are due to effusions
					A "joint effusion" is fluid contained WITHIN a body cavity
						Effusion commonly can accumulate inside synovial joint capsules
						The knee joint is the largest synovial cavity in the body
			-Fluid accumulation of this nature will present in global fashion all around the joint as shown here:
				("Fluid accumulation.png")
				The implications of the location of the swelling/fluid include:
					An intra-articular injury may cause intra-articular swelling
					However, an extra-articular injury likely won’t cause intra-articular swelling 
				-These special tests help gather additional information about the presence of fluid inside or surrounding the knee joint
		Stroke Test – AKA The Brush and Sweep Test (to Assess Joint Effusion) ("Brush & Sweep Test assessment.png")
			[MSK1_BrushTest]
			-The Brush & Sweep Test assesses for effusion that exists outside the knee capsule that is mostly situated in peri-patellar spaces. As this test is used to detect the presence of “minimal effusion” performed when the presence of effusion is suspected but not immediately obvious
			-To perform the Brush & Sweep Test, the therapist Attempts to sweep the effusion out of the medial sulcus of the knee and grades the amount that returns during a downward lateral stroke
			-Specifically, the therapist should
				1.Perform three upward strokes on the medial side of the knee
				2.Followed by one swift downward stroke on the lateral side
			Table 1: Effusion Grading Scale of the knee joint based on the Stroke Test ("Effusion Grading Scale.png")
				Grade + Test Result (!!E.Grade & Doc)
					Zero  - No wave produced on downstroke
					Trace - Small wave on medical side with downstroke
					1+    - Larger bulge on medial side with downstroke
					2+    - Effusion spontaneously returns to medial side after upstroke (no downstroke necessary)
					3+    - So much fluid that it is not possible to move the effusion out of the medical aspect of the knee
			The following video demonstrates performance of the Brush and Sweep Test.
				(See: "06_04 Brush and Sweep Test.mp4")
			This video shows what is observed in a positive BRUSH AND SWEEP Test.
				("06_05 Brush and Sweep Test Positive Result.mp4") (Tornar a gravar el video ¿?)
			------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
			-This test is performed with the patient in a supine position with the knee in full extension
			-First "brush" superiorly 3 times on the medial side of the knee joint (beginning just below the joint line and ending as far as the suprapatellar pouch) in attempt to push fluid laterally
			-Next "sweep" inferiorly the lateral side of the knee capsule
			-The test is positive if there is a bulging or movement of the fluid.
			-This test is used to detect subtle or minimal effusion (as little as 4-8 mm) around the knee joint.

				Grafts used for ACL Reconstruction (surgery)
					AUTOGRAFTS
						is when a section of the patient's own tissue is used to replace the ACL
						Commonly used grafts for this purpose include section of the patients:
							Patellar tendon
							Hamstring
							Quadriceps tendon
					ALLOGRAFTS 
						-Autografts are advantageous because they reduce the risk of the body rejecting internalization of the tissue and it recognizes it as its own so we don't get an infection. New surgical techniques have reduced this effect or the risk for infection or rejection for a patient who's predisposed to it. Due to other co-morbidities, however, this is still likely a good option. The use of autograft tissue, however, involves healing that must occur within the area from which the graft is taken. So for example, if a piece of a patellar tendon is taken away and used to replace the ACL, not only do we have to have healing inside the joint where the ACL is now grafted, but also the patellar tendon itself has to heal. For this reason, it's much more common that allografts or the use of an ACL harvested from a cadaver are implemented. Again, this is more common lately due to enhanced methods of graft fixation and decreased tissue rejection. It removes the need for the body to heal where a hamstring or patellar tendon has been removed as occurs in an autograft.
					XENOGRAFTS
						-It is when a ligament or other tissue from an animal such as a pig is used to replace the ACL. These are not common but if we have a patient which for some reason an allograft or an autograft is not an option, that might be something they would consider

				-If the patella becomes locked in this position, it is referred to as a dislocation and may need to be manually relocated, AKA reduced. But if it re-locates on its own, this is sometimes described as a subluxation``.
				-The common mechanism of injury for patellar instability or dislocation or subluxation is a
				valgus force direct to the knee with the knee in about 20°-30° of flexion.
					-The patient would commonly report that they felt a pop and their knee instantly gave out. And of course, they often can report that the kneecap was out of place.
				-Dislocations can result in damage to other tissues like as
					osteochondral cartilage,
					bone bruises, or
					osteochondritis dessicans.
				-Looking at patellar instability from a treatment perspective we want to
					-reduce pain and inflammation and
					-stabilize with the quadriceps and hamstrings
					-muscle balance
						later on to be able to really minimize the abnormal forces on the knee
					-And as with anything else at the knee, we're always going to focus on strengthening of hip musculature
					-Neuromuscular control and proprioception because
						-we really want the muscles to be recruited in a way that stabilizes the patella and minimizes its ability to move out of position

	Osteoarthritis (OA) of the Knee
		-This degenerative joint disease that involves breakdown of articular cartilage in the three compartments of knee (medial, lateral, patellofemoral). Of these, degenerative joint disease (DJD) of the medial compartment is most common
		-The stages of osteoarthritis of the knee joint based on severity of degradation ("Stages of Knee Osteoarthritis.png") (!!E.Grade & Doc)
			I Doubtful - Minimum disruption
				There is already 10% cartilage loss
			II Mild - Joint-space narrowing
				The cartilage to begin breaking down
				Occurrence of osteophytes
			III Moderate - Moderate joint-space reduction
				Gaps in the cartilage can expand until they reach the bone
			IV Severe - Joint-space greatly reduced
				60% of the cartilage is already lost
				Large osteophytes``

	-In addition, I noted the following patterns of misconceptions in the written questions about the patient post-TKA and the patient with PFPS
		-Many students mentioned the use of heat or ice to treat swelling. While ice would not be inappropriate it does not do much to remove existing swelling (there was no deduction for that). Applying heat on a swollen or inflamed area, however, can be detrimental and further increase inflammation.
		-Flexibility testing would be very low priority on the exam post-surgery as these would be difficult to perform on someone who lacks significant ROM. Also identifying tight muscles would be low priority as there are many other higher priority issues.
		-Same with proprioception testing. When lacking significant motion and likely using an assistive device, assessing this is a low priority early on.
		-Many mentioned strength testing of the hip flexors as a test to do at eval. As we have discussed, the hip flexors provide minimal stability in the lower extremity (i.e.; not steak muscles). Also they are used frequently due to the nature of human movement and less likely to become weak as compared to other hip muscles.
		-MMTs for knee flexion and knee extension would not be well-tolerated in a patient post-surgery and therefore not the best choices to perform.
		-Instability tests like varus, valgus, anterior drawer, etc. would not be appropriate for a patient post-TKA.  No reason to suspect instability in these patients as the ligaments they test are no longer present.  
		-The Brush and Sweep test is not used when swelling is obvious. It is only a test for minimal effusion – which means you are not certain if it is present or not with the naked eye. If swelling is obvious there is no additional value in performing it.
		-And finally, many students widely missed the point about selectively trying to strengthen the VMO and said that this is something that would be beneficial. As I mentioned in class 1) There is minimal evidence that any exercise can selectively target or recruit the VMO and 2) Even if you could, research where these exercises are compared to ones that target hip muscles shows that targeting hip muscles provides much superior outcomes. Take home message – Don’t try to selectively train the VMO!
