        1st Acute Inflamation Stage
            The Acute Inflammation Stage occurs one to 4 days after injury

            Occurs three to 20 days (3 weeks) after an injury
        3rd.Maturation and Remodeling Stage
            The Maturation and Remodeling Stage begins around 21 days (3 weeks) after an injury
            After 3 weeks, deposition of collagen fibers occurs in a much more organized fashion

(Γ)Classification of a patient's condition as acute, subacute, or chronic
    1. Acute
       - Duration: 0-4 weeks (0-30 days)
       - Characteristics:
         * Recent onset of symptoms
         * Tissue healing phase
         * High irritability
         * Rapid changes in symptoms

    2. Subacute
       - Duration: 4-12 weeks (1-3 months)
       - Characteristics:
         * Transition period between acute and chronic phases
         * Moderate irritability
         * Some improvement in symptoms, but not fully resolved

    3. Chronic
       - Duration: More than 12 weeks (>3 months)
       - Characteristics:
         * Persistent symptoms
         * Low irritability
         * Slower rate of change in symptoms
         * May involve central sensitization

    It's important to note that these timeframes are general guidelines and may vary slightly depending on the specific condition or the healthcare professional's approach. The classification helps in determining appropriate treatment strategies and understanding the nature of the patient's condition.

    Additionally, some sources may use slightly different cut-off points, such as considering 6 weeks as the transition from acute to subacute. However, the overall concept of progression from acute to chronic remains consistent.
