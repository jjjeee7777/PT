	### **Anatomical Structures in the Region of the Pubic Tubercle**

	#### **Structures Attaching to the Pubic Bone:**
	From lateral (out) to medial (in):
	1. **Muscles:**
	   - Pectineus muscle
	   - Adductor longus muscle
	   - Adductor brevis muscle
	   - Obturator externus muscle

	2. **Abdominal Muscles (Superior to the Pubic Bone):**
	   - External abdominal oblique muscle
	   - Internal abdominal oblique muscle
	   - Transversus abdominis muscle
	   - Pyramidalis muscle (if present)
	   - Rectus abdominis muscle
	   - Psoas minor muscle (if present)

	3. **Ligaments and Tendinous Structures:**
	   - Inguinal ligament (runs from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle)
	   - Linea alba (midline tendinous structure extending from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis)

	#### **Other Relevant Structures:**
	1. **Pubic Symphysis:**
	   - The cartilaginous joint uniting the left and right pubic bones.

	2. **Soft Tissue Surrounding the Pubic Rami:**
	   From lateral (out) to medial (in):
	   - **Muscles:**
	     - Iliacus muscle
	     - Psoas major muscle
	     - Iliopsoas muscle (formed by the union of the iliacus and psoas major muscles)
	   - **Bursa:**
	     - Iliopsoas (iliopectineal) bursa
	   - **Ligaments:**
	     - Pubofemoral ligament
	     - Iliofemoral ligament

	---

	### **Additional Clarifications:**
	1. **Above the Pubic Tubercle:**
	   - The abdominal wall muscles (external and internal oblique, transversus abdominis) attach indirectly to the pubic tubercle and surrounding structures via their aponeuroses.
	   - The rectus abdominis muscle inserts on the pubic crest and pubic symphysis.

	2. **Posterior to the Pubic Bone:**
	   - Obturator internus and the pelvic diaphragm may also be considered if extending the regional focus.

	3. **Variations:**
	   - The pyramidalis muscle and psoas minor muscle are not consistently present in all individuals.

	This revision provides a clear, organized approach, grouping structures by their anatomical relevance and proximity to the pubic tubercle.
