                     ## 3º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT TO RULE OUT BPPV

                     Now, we **rule out BPPV** using **positional testing**.

                     ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: What Is BPPV?

                     - **Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)** is a **peripheral vestibular disorder**.
                     - Caused by **otoconia** (calcium crystals) in semicircular canals.
                     - Most common: **Posterior canal BPPV** → **geotropic torsional upbeating nystagmus** on Dix-Hallpike.

                     > 🔄 Compare:  
                     > - **Horizontal canal BPPV** → **horizontal nystagmus**  
                     > - **Central positional nystagmus** → **downbeating, no latency, no fatigue**

                     ---

                     ### ✅ TESTS TO RULE OUT BPPV

                     1. **Dix-Hallpike Maneuver**  
                        - *Purpose*: Diagnose **posterior canal BPPV**  
                        - *How*:  
                          - Sit → quickly lay back with head 20° below table, turned 45° to one side  
                          - **Feet must be on the ground or supported** ← SAFETY ISSUE FROM YOUR FEEDBACK  
                          - Observe for **nystagmus and vertigo**  
                        - *Positive if*:  
                          - **Latency** (1–5 sec delay)  
                          - **Rotational (torsional) + upbeating nystagmus**  
                          - **Fatigues with repetition**  
                          - **Vertigo matches symptoms**

                     2. **Supine Roll Test**  
                        - *Purpose*: Diagnose **horizontal canal BPPV**  
                        - *How*: Lie on back → rapidly roll head 90° left/right  
                        - *Positive if*: **horizontal nystagmus**, beats toward lower ear (geotropic) or upper ear (ageotropic)

                     > ⚠️ Safety: **Feet on ground or supported** during all tests. You were marked down for this.

                        ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: The Neurologic Exam Framework

                        The **Neurologic Exam** is not a checklist — it’s a **hierarchical, hypothesis-driven process**.

                        We follow this order:

                        1. **Mental Status** → Can the patient participate?
                        2. **Cranial Nerves** → Especially II, III, IV, VI, VIII (vision, eye movement, hearing/balance)

                        So we don’t test balance before checking if the patient can follow commands.

                        | Type | What It Assesses | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                        |------|------------------|-------------|-------------|
                        | **Static Standing Balance** | Sensory orientation (vision, proprioception, vestibular) | Use **Romberg, Sharpened Romberg** | If impaired, we don’t progress to dynamic tasks |
                        | **Dynamic Standing Balance** | Self-generated postural control | Use **Tandem Walk, Step Test** | Shows ability to adapt to movement |
                        | **Walking Balance** | Integration of systems during locomotion | Use **Functional Gait Assessment (FGA)** | More sensitive than 10MWT for vestibular patients |

                        > 🔄 Compare:  
                        > - In **BPPV**, balance is **cautious but intact** when still  
                        > - In **cerebellar ataxia**, it’s **wide-based, irregular, with truncal sway**

                        We use **standardized tools** (FGA, Berg) because they’re **reliable, valid, and track change**.

                        #### 4.d. **Mental Status Exam**

                        Quick screen:
                        - **Alertness**: Is she awake? Oriented?
                        - **Attention**: Can she follow 2-step commands?
                        - *Why?* If not alert, we **cannot proceed** with vestibular rehab — may need referral.

                        We don’t do full MoCA unless cognitive concerns — too long for screening.

                        ---

                        #### 5.e. **Motor Exam**

                        Key elements:

                        | Element | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                        |--------|---------------|-------------|
                        | **Muscle Mass** | Look for atrophy (e.g., from inactivity) | If weak, may need strengthening before VOR |
                        | **ROM (Cervical)** | Limited rotation → cervicogenic dizziness | If stiff, may need manual therapy |
                        | **Tone** | Spasticity → UMN. Rigidity → Parkinson’s | Abnormal tone changes diagnosis |
                        | **Strength (MMT)** | Leg and neck strength → affects balance | Weakness = fall risk |
                        | **Involuntary Movements** | Tremor, nystagmus | Nystagmus at rest → central lesion |

                        > 🔁 You were marked down for not identifying **motor component** — so we are now **explicitly including it** and explaining **why**.

                        ---

                        #### 6.f. **Functional Skills Exam and Movement Analysis**

                        Observe:
                        - **Sit-to-Stand**: Uses arms? Slow initiation?  
                        - **Transfers**: Needs help?  
                        - **Walking**: Head stiff? Wide base?  
                        - **Stairs**: Can she do them?

                        Use **Movement Analysis Worksheet** to note:
                        - Head-trunk dissociation
                        - Step length asymmetry
                        - Trunk stiffness

                        > Why? Because **functional movement reveals compensation patterns**.

                        ---

                        #### 7.g. **Cranial Nerve Integrity Exam**

                        Prioritize:
                        - **CN II (Optic)**: Visual acuity, visual fields  
                        - **CN III, IV, VI**: Extraocular movements (H-test), saccades, smooth pursuit  
                        - **CN VIII (Vestibulocochlear)**: Hearing (whisper test), VOR (HIT), nystagmus

                        > Why? Because **eye movements and vestibular function are linked** via the **vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR)**.

                        We don’t test CN V or VII unless facial symptoms — not relevant here.

                        ---

                        #### 8.h. **Coordination Exam**

                        | Test | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                        |------|---------------|-------------|
                        | **Finger-to-Nose (FTN)** | Check for dysmetria → cerebellar lesion | If abnormal, suggests central vertigo |
                        | **Heel-to-Shin (HTS)** | Same | Differentiates central from peripheral |
                        | **Rapid Alternating Movements (RAM)** | Dysdiadochokinesia → cerebellar | If impaired, we refer |

                        > 🔄 Compare:  
                        > - In **BPPV**, coordination is **normal**  
                        > - In **stroke or MS**, it’s **impaired**

                        So we do this to **rule out central pathology**.

                        ## 3º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT TO RULE OUT BPPV

                        Now, we **test for BPPV** — but why this test and not another?

                        ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: The Vestibular System

                        The **inner ear** has:
                        - **Semicircular canals** → detect **rotational head movement**
                        - **Utricle & Saccule** → detect **linear acceleration and gravity**

                        In **BPPV**, otoconia (crystals) break off and enter a canal — most commonly the **posterior canal**.

                        When the head moves, they move → false signal → vertigo + nystagmus.

                        So we **reproduce the trigger** with **Dix-Hallpike**.

                        ---

                        ### ✅ Dix-Hallpike Maneuver

                        | Step | Why We Do It | Why Not Do It Differently |
                        |------|---------------|--------------------------|
                        | 1. Sit → lay back fast, head 20° below table, turned 45° | Aligns posterior canal with gravity | If head not below table, canal not vertical — test fails |
                        | 2. Feet on ground or supported | Safety! You were marked down for this | Without support, patient may fall during vertigo |
                        | 3. Observe for 30 sec | Latency and duration matter | Stopping early misses crescendo-decrescendo pattern |
                        | 4. Look for torsional upbeating nystagmus toward lower ear | Classic for posterior canal BPPV | Horizontal = horizontal canal; downbeating = central |

                        > 🔄 Compare:  
                        > - **BPPV**: Latency, fatigues, torsional-upbeating  
                        > - **Central positional nystagmus**: No latency, persists, downbeating

                        So we do Dix-Hallpike because it’s **specific for posterior canal BPPV** — and we don’t use Supine Roll Test first because that’s for **horizontal canal**.

                        ---

                        ## 4º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT: VOMS

                        Now, broader screening: **Vestibular/Ocular Motor Screening (VOMS)**

                        | Test | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                        |------|---------------|-------------|
                        | **Smooth Pursuits** | Saccadic tracking → cerebellar/brainstem | If abnormal, suggests central lesion |
                        | **Saccades** | Overshoot/undershoot → frontal lobe | Helps differentiate BPPV from stroke |
                        | **Convergence** | Double vision <5 cm → CN III or midbrain | If impaired, may need referral |
                        | **VOR (HIT)** | Catch-up saccade → vestibular hypofunction | In BPPV, VOR is **normal** — so if abnormal, it’s not BPPV |
                        | **Positional Testing** | Already done (Dix-Hallpike) | Confirms diagnosis |

                        > Why not do all 10 cranial nerves? Because only **II, III, IV, VI, VIII** are relevant to dizziness.
