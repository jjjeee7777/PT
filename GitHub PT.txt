                     ## 3º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT TO RULE OUT BPPV

                     Now, we **rule out BPPV** using **positional testing**.

                     ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: What Is BPPV?

                     - **Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)** is a **peripheral vestibular disorder**.
                     - Caused by **otoconia** (calcium crystals) in semicircular canals.
                     - Most common: **Posterior canal BPPV** → **geotropic torsional upbeating nystagmus** on Dix-Hallpike.

                     > 🔄 Compare:  
                     > - **Horizontal canal BPPV** → **horizontal nystagmus**  
                     > - **Central positional nystagmus** → **downbeating, no latency, no fatigue**

                     ---

                     ### ✅ TESTS TO RULE OUT BPPV

                     1. **Dix-Hallpike Maneuver**  
                        - *Purpose*: Diagnose **posterior canal BPPV**  
                        - *How*:  
                          - Sit → quickly lay back with head 20° below table, turned 45° to one side  
                          - **Feet must be on the ground or supported** ← SAFETY ISSUE FROM YOUR FEEDBACK  
                          - Observe for **nystagmus and vertigo**  
                        - *Positive if*:  
                          - **Latency** (1–5 sec delay)  
                          - **Rotational (torsional) + upbeating nystagmus**  
                          - **Fatigues with repetition**  
                          - **Vertigo matches symptoms**

                     2. **Supine Roll Test**  
                        - *Purpose*: Diagnose **horizontal canal BPPV**  
                        - *How*: Lie on back → rapidly roll head 90° left/right  
                        - *Positive if*: **horizontal nystagmus**, beats toward lower ear (geotropic) or upper ear (ageotropic)

                     > ⚠️ Safety: **Feet on ground or supported** during all tests. You were marked down for this.

                        ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: The Neurologic Exam Framework

                        The **Neurologic Exam** is not a checklist — it’s a **hierarchical, hypothesis-driven process**.

                        We follow this order:

                        1. **Mental Status** → Can the patient participate?
                        2. **Cranial Nerves** → Especially II, III, IV, VI, VIII (vision, eye movement, hearing/balance)

                        So we don’t test balance before checking if the patient can follow commands.

                        ## 3º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT TO RULE OUT BPPV

                        Now, we **test for BPPV** — but why this test and not another?

                        ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: The Vestibular System

                        The **inner ear** has:
                        - **Semicircular canals** → detect **rotational head movement**
                        - **Utricle & Saccule** → detect **linear acceleration and gravity**

                        ### ✅ Dix-Hallpike Maneuver

                        | Step | Why We Do It | Why Not Do It Differently |
                        |------|---------------|--------------------------|
                        | 1. Sit → lay back fast, head 20° below table, turned 45° | Aligns posterior canal with gravity | If head not below table, canal not vertical — test fails |

                        | 3. Observe for 30 sec | Latency and duration matter | Stopping early misses crescendo-decrescendo pattern |
                        | 4. Look for torsional upbeating nystagmus toward lower ear | Classic for posterior canal BPPV | Horizontal = horizontal canal; downbeating = central |

                        > 🔄 Compare:  
                        > - **BPPV**: Latency, fatigues, torsional-upbeating  
                        > - **Central positional nystagmus**: No latency, persists, downbeating

                     ## 2º. WHAT WOULD YOU PRIORITIZE IN THE NEUROLOGIC EXAM?

                     Now, based on history, we **prioritize** the exam.

                     But first — why not test everything?

                     ### 🔍 BIG PICTURE: The Neurologic Exam Hierarchy

                     We follow a **hierarchical order** because higher systems **enable** lower ones:

                     1. **Mental Status** → Can the patient participate?  
                     2. **Cranial Nerves** → Especially **II, III, IV, VI, VIII** (vision, eye movement, balance)  
                     3. **Sensory** → Proprioception, light touch  
                     4. **Motor** → Strength, tone, coordination  
                     5. **Balance & Coordination** → Static, dynamic, walking  
                     6. **Functional Skills** → Sit-to-stand, gait, transfers

                     > ✅ Why this order?  
                     > - If the patient is confused, balance testing is invalid.  
                     > - If they have gaze palsy, VOR testing won’t work.

                     So we don’t jump to gait — we build from the top down.

                     ---

                     ### ✅ PRIORITIZED EXAM COMPONENTS — AND WHY

                     #### 2.a. **History, Observation, and Review of Systems (ROS)**

                     We already did the history.

                     Now, **observation**:
                     - Watch how she walks in: wide base? Trunk sway? Holding onto walls?  
                     - Does she avoid head movements?  
                     - *Why?* Natural behavior reveals **compensatory strategies**.

                     **ROS Screening**:
                     - **Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic BP? (dizziness on standing)  
                       - *Why?* Could mimic vestibular disorder  
                       - *Why not skip?* If BP drops, it’s not AUVP — it’s orthostasis
                     - **Neuromuscular:** Any tremor, rigidity?  
                       - *Why?* Could suggest Parkinson’s or cerebellar disease  

                     > 🔁 You were marked down for not identifying **motor or vestibular components** — so we are now **explicitly including** them.

                     ---

                     #### 2.b. **Sensory Exam**

                     We focus on:
                     - **Light Touch (LT)** – Screen for peripheral neuropathy  
                     - **Proprioception (Joint Position Sense)** – Big toe up/down  

                     > 🔍 Why? To rule out **sensory ataxia** as a contributor.

                     > 🔄 Compare:  
                     > - In **AUVP**, proprioception is **intact** — imbalance is vestibular  
                     > - In **bilateral vestibulopathy**, patient becomes **dependent on proprioception**  
                     > - In **dorsal column lesion**, proprioception is **absent** → sensory ataxia

                     So we do this to **rule out alternative causes of imbalance**.

                     ---

                     #### 3.c. **Balance Exam**

                     This is **core** to vestibular rehab.

                     | Type | Test | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                     |------|------|-------------|-------------|
                     | **Static Standing Balance** | Romberg, Sharpened Romberg | Assess sensory integration | In AUVP, Romberg may be **positive** due to poor vestibular input |
                     | **Dynamic Standing Balance** | Tandem Walk, Step Test | Self-generated control | Shows ability to adapt to movement |
                     | **Walking Balance** | Functional Gait Assessment (FGA) | Most sensitive for vestibular patients | Includes head turns, eyes closed, obstacle clearance |

                     > ⚠️ Safety: **Feet on ground, hands ready to guard** — she’s dizzy.

                     ---

                     #### 4.d. **Mental Status Exam**

                     Quick screen:
                     - **Alertness**: Is she awake? Oriented?  
                     - **Attention**: Can she follow 2-step commands?  
                     - **Speech**: Is it slurred?  
                     - *Why?* To ensure she can **participate** in rehab.

                     We don’t do full MoCA unless concerns — too long for screening.

                     ---

                     #### 5.e. **Motor Exam**

                     Key elements:

                     | Element | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                     |--------|---------------|-------------|
                     | **Muscle Mass** | Look for atrophy (e.g., from inactivity) | Not acute, but important for long-term plan |
                     | **ROM (Cervical)** | Limited rotation → cervicogenic dizziness | Rule out |
                     | **Tone** | **Normal** in AUVP | Spasticity → UMN. Rigidity → Parkinson’s |
                     | **Strength (MMT)** | Check for weakness | AUVP doesn’t cause weakness — if present, think central |
                     | **Involuntary Movements** | Nystagmus, tremor | Spontaneous nystagmus = vestibular |

                     > 🔁 You were marked down for not identifying **motor component** — so we are now **explicitly including it**.

                     ---

                     #### 6.f. **Functional Skills Exam and Movement Analysis**

                     Observe:
                     - **Sit-to-Stand**: Uses arms? Slow initiation?  
                     - **Transfers**: Needs help?  
                     - **Walking**: Wide base? Head stiff?  
                     - **Stairs**: Can she do them?

                     Use **Movement Analysis Worksheet** to note:
                     - Head-trunk dissociation
                     - Step length asymmetry
                     - Trunk stiffness

                     > Why? Because **functional movement reveals compensation patterns**.

                     ---

                     #### 7.g. **Cranial Nerve Integrity Exam**

                     Prioritize:
                     - **CN II (Optic)**: Visual acuity, fields  
                     - **CN III, IV, VI**: Extraocular movements (H-test), saccades, smooth pursuit  
                     - **CN VIII (Vestibulocochlear)**: Hearing (whisper test), VOR (HIT), nystagmus

                     > Why? Because **eye movements and vestibular function are linked** via the **VOR**.

                     We don’t test CN V or VII unless facial symptoms — not relevant here.

                     ---

                     #### 8.h. **Coordination Exam**

                     | Test | Why We Do It | Why Not Skip |
                     |------|---------------|-------------|
                     | **Finger-to-Nose (FTN)** | Check for dysmetria → cerebellar lesion | In AUVP, coordination is **normal** |
                     | **Heel-to-Shin (HTS)** | Same | Differentiates central from peripheral |
                     | **Rapid Alternating Movements (RAM)** | Dysdiadochokinesia → cerebellar | If impaired, we refer |

                     > 🔄 Compare:  
                     > - In **AUVP**, coordination is **normal**  
                     > - In **stroke or MS**, it’s **impaired**

                     So we do this to **rule out central pathology**.

                     ## 3º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT TO RULE OUT BPPV

                     Even though this is likely AUVP, we **must rule out BPPV** because:
                     - BPPV can coexist
                     - Misdiagnosis leads to wrong treatment

                     ### ✅ Tests to Rule Out BPPV

                     1. **Dix-Hallpike Maneuver**  
                        - *Purpose*: Diagnose posterior canal BPPV  
                        - *How*: Sit → lay back, head 20° below table, turned 45°  
                        - *Positive if*: Latency, torsional-upbeating nystagmus, fatigues  
                        - *In this case*: **No latency, no fatigue, no vertigo** → **not BPPV**

                     2. **Supine Roll Test**  
                        - *Purpose*: Diagnose horizontal canal BPPV  
                        - *In this case*: **No horizontal nystagmus** → not BPPV

                     > ⚠️ Safety: **Feet on ground or supported** — she’s dizzy.

                     ## 4º. DEMONSTRATE AN ASSESSMENT: VOMS

                     Now, broader screening: **Vestibular/Ocular Motor Screening (VOMS)**

                     | Test | Expected Finding in AUVP | Why We Do It |
                     |------|--------------------------|-------------|
                     | **Smooth Pursuits** | **Normal** | AUVP doesn’t affect oculomotor control |
                     | **Saccades** | **Normal** | Impaired in central lesions |
                     | **Convergence** | Normal | Midbrain involvement? |
                     | **VOR (HIT)** | **Positive (catch-up saccade)** | Indicates **ipsilateral vestibular hypofunction** |
                     | **Positional Testing** | No nystagmus | Rules out BPPV |

                     > 🔄 Compare:  
                     > - In **BPPV**: VOR **normal**, Dix-Hallpike **positive**  
                     > - In **AUVP**: VOR **abnormal**, Dix-Hallpike **negative**
