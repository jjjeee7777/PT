⇅Overlap with the Knee (*See "Hip and Knee Flexibility Tests") REPE
	-Muscles crossing the hip joint provide significant stability and control for the knee REPE
	-Lack of flexibility in soft tissues around the hip may influence knee mechanics and tight knee structures may in turn influence hip movements REPE
	-Assess the flexibility of structures crossing the hip and/or knee joint when evaluating a hip
Unique
	-Must be extremely strong in order to sustain the weight of the entire body while remaining exceedingly flexible to permit multi-planar movement
		Loss of either or both greatly impairs function

History and Screening Considerations for the Hip [MSK1_C3.4]
	Location *See [MSK1_L05_ReferredPainLoc]
		-As mentioned earlier, the location of symptoms around the hip may vary greatly due to the
			high degree of converging nerve fibers and
			depth of anatomical structures.
		-Although, a diagnosis should never be based solely on location,
			symptoms in certain locations are more commonly associated with specific conditions. 
		-See the types of conditions that commonly present in that location ("Hip Conditions By Location.png")
			Anterior
				Symptoms presenting here tend to be associated with pathology affecting the
					joint surfaces
					hip flexors
					iliopsoas bursa
			Lateral
				Symptoms presenting here tend to be associated with
					gluteal pain syndrome
						greater trochanteric bursitis
						gluteus medius pain syndrome
			Posterior (REFERRED)
				This is the least common referral pattern of the hip but when present more likely associated with pathology of the pathology of
					hip extensors, or
					hip external rotators
			Groin (REFERRED)
				Refers from multiple hip anatomy structures; No clear pattern.
	Exacerbating Factors
		As with any region, the patient should be questioned about specific exacerbating activities. 
		Those of most interest for the hip include:
			Is there pain with weight-bearing? If so, does this come on with: 
				Stairs, walking, running, squatting? 
			Is there pain at rest? 
				Pain at rest can be associated with higher levels of
					inflammation,
					severity or
					irritability 
				If not altered by position or activity, may also be indicative of a systemic condition
	----------------------------------------------------------------
	Medical and Developmental History
		A patient’s medical and developmental history is often relevant to the onset of hip pathology. 
		Specifically, the PT should ask about: 
			Previous injuries of the
				hip
				back or
				other portions of the lower extremities 
			Breach position during childbirth, as this can cause issues during growth and development 
			Childhood disorders such as:
				Developmental dysplasia of the hip ("Developmental Hip Dysplasia.png")
				Legg-Calve-Perthes disease 
				Slipped capital femoral epiphysis
	-----------------------------------------------------------------
	Screening Considerations for the Hip and Pelvic Region
		-Because differential diagnosis of the hip region can be less straight-forward than other regions in the lower extremity, screening for ...↓ is an important component.
			red flag conditions and [MSK1_C3.4.1]
				As in other regions, the report of certain symptoms require the red flag conditions be ruled out. 
					Examples include:
						Acute hip pain with fever
						Night sweats
						Night pain
						Symptoms unchanged by position or movement
						Symptoms related to the onset of the menstrual cycle
				Red flag conditions that can present as a typical hip pathology are listed below.
				Identification of these necessitates a timely referral/consultation.
					----------------------------------------------
					Systemic Conditions
						Cancer/neoplasm
						Septic conditions/infection
						Crohn’s disease
						Urinary tract infection
						Appendix rupture
						Osteomyelitis
						Endometrioses
					Serious Musculoskeletal Conditions
						Fracture (femoral or pelvic)
						Avascular necrosis
						Legg-Calve-Perthes disease
						Slipped capital femoral epiphysis
				------------------------------------------
				Pelvic Floor Pathologies
					Lastly, physical therapists should be aware that pathology of the pelvic floor may present as hip pathology.
						-Indicators of pelvic floor dysfunction may include:
							Urinary difficulties
							Evacuation difficulties
							Sexual dysfunction
							Symptoms fluctuating with the menstrual cycle
							These often accompany musculoskeletal symptoms such as hip and lumbopelvic pain
						-It is important to remember that urinary incontinence, pelvic pain, and girdle dysfunction are not exclusive to females.
							-However, the unique anatomy and specific life events such as pregnancy, delivery, and menopause predispose the female population to pelvic floor concerns.
			involvement of surrounding structures or regions
		--------------------------------------------
		Goals of the screening process for patients with hip and pelvic issues include: 
			Identify patients in need of immediate referral
				i.e.: those with red flag conditions
			Identify patients of "concern"
				Is additional diagnostic testing necessary to rule out red flag conditions?
				Is future referral to another provider necessary?
			Determine single vs. multiple regions of involvement
				Is there involvement of one or multiple hip structures?
			Prioritize further examination approach for adjacent regions
				Symptoms in the hip region may originate from the following regions that should be ruled out
					(See [MSK1_HipTriage])
					----------------------
					Pelvic girdle
					Lumbosacral complex
					Possibly the knee

		Purpose for Flexibility Testing
			1.Assess possible asymmetries left vs. right
			
			2.Assess for lack of flexibility on BOTH sides
			3.Determine if absence of flexibility may be related to or cause "biomechanical faults"
				-If present may cause uneven stresses on tissues throughout the lower extremity or on the spine that are ultimately the source of a patient's problem. And, if bio-mechanical faults are present, that could be something that can lead to their ultimate pathology or problems
			4.Guide treatment
				-If we see a lack of flexibility and that lack of flexibility is associated with the patient's problems, we probably want to provide interventions or prescribe interventions that restore length of these tissues.

	The Treatment-Based Classification (TBC) Approach
		-Prior to discussing specific pathologies affecting the hip joint, it is important to consider the evidence-based strategy for diagnosing and managing hip conditions. This includes: 
			1HipOver.A review of the Treatment-Based Classification (TBC) Approach.
				-Contemporary research suggests that most conditions affecting the "musculoskeletal system" are best managed using this approach.
				-Treatment-Based Classification (TBC) is a model whereby the clinician makes treatment decisions based on the patient's clinical presentation. The primary purpose of the TBC approach is to identify baseline patient characteristics that predict responsiveness to varying treatment strategies
				-------------------------------------------
				-Treatment-Based Classification Approach (Γ TBCA) vs. Treatment-Based on Patho-Anatomical Diagnosis AKA Classification-Based Treatment (Γ TBP-AD)
					The TBC approach exists in contrast to the patho-anatomical model
						Patho-Anatomical Model (Γ Quina és la estructura que causa els efectes que vejem)
							-which focuses on the specific anatomical structures that are thought to be injured, damaged, or otherwise affected. 
								I.e.:
									herniated lumbar disc,
									a tear of the medial meniscus,or
									trochanteric bursitis
							-Advocates of this approach commonly select treatments based on protocols or regimens perceived as effective for patients with the associated diagnosis
						-TBC Approach (Γ Quins són els efectes que vejem, el resultat que dona la estructura danyà + la estructura danyà)
							-It focuses on matching interventions with the patient’s signs, symptoms, and impaired body structures identified during the history and physical exam
								Examples of findings that might guide a TBC-focused plan of care could include:
									joint hypermobility
									pain that radiates into an extremity
									impairments of muscle coordination

															(Γ)Why stretches to maximize flexibility help on the treatment of Greater trochanteric pain syndrome?
																Flexibility exercises can help address muscle tightness and imbalance, which are common contributors to GTPS. By improving flexibility, the tension and stress on the affected muscles and tendons, such as the hip abductors and extensors, can be reduced, potentially alleviating pain and discomfort associated with GTPS. Additionally, stretches can help enhance the overall range of motion in the hip joint, promoting better movement patterns and reducing the risk of abnormal stress on the greater trochanteric region. This can be particularly important for individuals with GTPS, as studies have suggested that abnormal control of lower limb movements and deficient neuromuscular parameters may contribute to the development of GTPS. Therefore, incorporating stretches to maximize flexibility as part of a comprehensive treatment plan for GTPS can help address underlying musculoskeletal issues and promote improved hip function and comfort.
														Strengthening of Abductors and Extensors
															Eccentric exercises?
															(Γ)-Why strengthening of Abductors and Extensors help on the treatment of Greater trochanteric pain syndrome?
																-Due to their role in hip stability and function. Research has indicated that GTPS may be related to poor hip and pelvic control, and strengthening the abductor and extensor muscles, especially in positions of unilateral support such as gait, can help address this issue. The hip abductors, including the gluteus medius and minimus, play a crucial role in stabilizing the pelvis during activities like walking and running. Weakness in these muscles can lead to increased pelvic drop on the opposite side during gait, potentially contributing to GTPS. Similarly, the hip extensors, such as the gluteus maximus, are important for activities like standing from a seated position and walking uphill. Strengthening these muscles can help improve overall hip stability and function, which may alleviate symptoms associated with GTPS. 		4.Avascular Necrosis
			-Avascular necrosis of the hip is a degenerative condition that results in decreased blood flow to proximal femur. Over time, this leads to structural failure and/or collapse of the bony surfaces that articulate within the hip
			-While genetics may predispose and individual to avascular necrosis, various direct and indirect factors place individuals at greater risk. These include:
				Direct Risk Factors
					History of fracture or dislocation
					History of a slipped capital femoral epiphysis
						("Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE).pdf")
					Radiation treatment
					Sickle cell disease
				Indirect Risk Factors
					Long term corticosteroid utilization
					Chronic alcohol abuse
					Autoimmune disease
					Smoking history
			Avascular Necrosis Radiologic Findings
				("Avascular Necrosis Radiologic Findings.png")
				1.Decreased Joint Space
					Decreased joint space in the right hip
				2.Space between femur and acetabulum
					Note the additional space between the femur and acetabulum on the left hand side
				3.Dense Coloration
					-More dense coloration in the head of the femur on the right as compared to the left. The increased signal indicates more densely packed bone created by structural failure

		C.1.Assesses dermatomes L1–S2
			Proper Performance during testing
			Able to identify 2-3 dermatome levels when asked
			Compares bilaterally immediately following opposite side
			Describes Rationale for Testing and Presentation of Abnormal Findings
				-A dermatome is the sensory innervation of spinal nerve roots and it is used to identify Nerve Root pathology and not peripheral nerve lesions (for DTRs is the same).
				-Dermatomes are areas of the skin that are innervated by the sensory fibers of a single spinal nerve. They are used to determine whether the sensory loss on a limb corresponds to a specific nerve root, which can help diagnose conditions such as radiculopathy. Diseases affecting dermatomes include radiculopathies, which can result from conditions such as disc herniation or trauma to the back. Symptoms that follow a dermatome, such as pain, tingling, numbness or a rash, may indicate a pathology involving the related nerve root.
