.1.17. Lumbar Spine Exam Summary Γ(Why are we doing these next steps?)
	Identifying common patterns of signs and symptoms with which patients present
	After completing this lesson, you should be able to recall that: 
		-Performing an examination for the lumbar spine includes similar categories of tests and measures as in other body regions.  
		-These test and measures are just specific to lumbar spine structures. As with other areas, screening should be performed to rule out the presence of serious red flag conditions.
		-A primary focus of the lumbar exam is determining what actions or motions provoke or relieve the patient’s familiar symptoms.

.(1.8.)Performing a systematic physical examination
	General Guidelines for the Physical Exam Γ(Things to consider before start)
		The following factors should guide how you conduct your physical exam:
			Establish baseline symptoms before you start movement testing
			Seek (in most cases) to reproduce a comparable sign
				-Movement that reproduces patient’s familiar symptom
					-The ability to identify a comparable sign associated with the patient’s familiar symptoms has been associated with a better prognosis.
				-Multiple symptoms may = multiple comparable signs.
			Plan the vigor of your assessment through tests and measures based on the patient’s identified SINSS.

    (1.10.)Lower Quarter Screening (LQS) (See [MSK1_LQS])(!!)Review
    	When appropriate, perform a lower quarter screening (LQS).
    		A LQS is performed when neurological involvement is SUSPECTED or POSSIBLE but not certain.
    			Standard components of the LQS include:
    				Sensation/Dermatome Testing 
    					Sensory innervation of spinal nerve roots.
    				Myotome Testing
    					Motor innervation of spinal nerve roots. 
    				Deep Tendon Reflexes
    					Assessment of the involuntary reflex loop between musculotendinous structures and the spinal cord.
    		If neurological involvement is CERTAIN then screening is omitted and detailed neuromuscular exam is performed.
    	('02 Mod1 LowerQuarterScreen.mp4')
    		PLEASE NOTE:
    			-The following video describes sensation screening using both Key ASIA (American Spinal Cord Association) Points AND sensation testing using longer strokes covering representative areas within each dermatome. Circumstances under which each are appropriate will be discussed further during live sessions but as we learned in MSK 1, it is typically more useful to screen sensation using the longer representative strokes within each dermatome. 
    		As a review, this involves:
    			-Performing 6-8" Γ(15.24 to 20.32 cm) strokes in similar areas on right and left sides of the body
    			-Performing multiple strokes within each dermatome, with the goal being to assess sensation of the skin in the MAJORITY of the dermatome
    				-i.e., you do not need to assess every millimeter of skin - just enough to be sure you are not missing any large areas)
    			-With the patient's eyes closed, asking him/her if they note any differences in sensation from side to side

    		.1.11.1. Variations of Lumbar Motion Testing
    			-Variations of lumbar motion testing include:
    				1.Quadrant Testing
    				2.Repeated Movement Testing
    				3.Passive Lumbar Motion Testing (See [MSK2_1.12.Passive Lumbar Motion Testing])
    				4.Accessory Lumbar Motion Testing (See ¿?)
    			-Review the following information for each of the variation tests. Please note that passive and accessory lumbar motion tests will be covered on the next page. 
    				1.Quadrant Testing (Combined Movement Testing) ('combined movement testing of the lumbar spine.png')
    					-Quadrant testing, also known as combined movement testing of the lumbar spine, is another way to attempt to provoke the patient’s symptoms. This is especially helpful when the patient’s symptoms are not provoked with straight plane movements of flexion, extension and side bend. 
    						-Common combined movements include either extension combined with side bend left or right (pictured below) or flexion combined with side bending​. 
    					-Variations on interpretation:
    						-SB & Ext same side painful: Possible tissue/joint compression? 
    						-SB / Ext opposite side painful: Possible tissue stretch? 
    						-Most accurate interpretation? Simply another way to provoke sxs in addition to cardinal plane movement or when cardinal plane motion is normal.
    				2.Repeated Movement Testing ('2024-05-07 12_05_51-StrokesPlus')
    					-Repeated movement testing is most appropriate for patients who report a pattern of change or variability in the location of their symptoms.
    						Peripheralization: Pain moving further away from the spine
    						Centralization: Pain moving closer to the spine
    							Note that intensity of pain is not the key here in identifying a movement preference. 
    					Repeated Movement (RM) Assessment 
    						-McKenzie examination protocol calls for repeated movements of multiple reps to assess for worsening/peripheralization of symptoms. 
    					Considerations for RM testing: 
    							Let history, SINSS, and rest of exam guide: 
    								Number of reps/sets
    								Position in which you test
    								Centralization typically more important to identify than peripheralization
    					Repeated movement testing variations 
    						The following are examples of possible positions in which to test repeated extension: 
    							Prone lying (positioning) 
    							Prone on elbows 
    							Prone on hands 
    							Extension in plantigrade 
    							Extension in standing (partial) 
    							Extension in standing (full)
    			Knowledge Check
    				-Repeated movement testing in a given direction should be implemented into the treatment plan when that movement causes: 
    					More pain
    					Less pain
    					Peripheralization of symptoms 
    					Centralization of symptoms

    					Centralization of symptoms
    						Repeated movement should be implemented into the treatment plan when the movement causes symptoms to be focused closer to the spine (centralization) and not when they radiate (peripheralization). Note that decreased pain is not necessarily the goal, but rather to see which movements reduce peripheralization.

    				-Which of the following situations is an example of centralization of symptoms in a patient who has low back pain and tingling and numbness that goes down the posterior thigh to the knee? 
    					Repeated flexion increases the intensity of the tingling and numbness 
    					Repeated extension does not change the tingling and numbness 
    					Repeated flexion reduces numbness in the thigh but intensifies pain across the waistline 
    					Repeated extension causes back pain to move into the buttock and thigh  
    					None of these are an example of centralization 

    					Repeated flexion reduces numbness in the thigh but intensifies pain across the waistline
    						While the other options all either represent no change or further peripheralization, this option is the only example of centralization. Note that even though pain is increased in the low back, it is still considered centralization because distal symptoms are lessened.
    1.12. Passive (PROM) and Accessory Motion Testing for the Lumbar Spine [MSK2_1.12.Passive Lumbar Motion Testing]
    	Physiologic PROM/Segmental Mobility Testing
    		What is meant by physiologic motion?
    			Physiologic Motion
    				-Physiologic motion is global movement – which can be assessed passively in the lumbar spine in a sidelying position​.
    				-With the patient in this position, the therapist can leverage his or her body to see and feel the effects of:
    					Flexion
    					Extension

    				(Γ)-------------------------------------------------------
    				"SnOut" and "SpPin" are mnemonics used in medicine to describe the properties of diagnostic tests:

    				1. "SnOut": (¦Si vullc que siga sensible, xo no està)
    				   - "SnOut" stands for "Sensitive, Negative rules Out." 
    				   - This means that a test with high sensitivity (ability to correctly identify those with the condition) and a negative result effectively rules out the presence of the condition.
    				   - In other words, if the test accurately detects the absence of the condition when it's truly not there, it's considered helpful for ruling out the condition.
    				   - In the context of the Straight Leg Raise (SLR) test for lumbar herniated nucleus pulposus (HNP) with radiculopathy, "SnOut" suggests that SLR, being sensitive (picking up most cases of the condition), is useful for ruling out lumbar HNP with radiculopathy when the test result is negative.

    				2. "SpPin":
    				   - "SpPin" stands for "Specific, Positive rules In." (¦Especialment tú no, xo ahí estàs)
    				   - This means that a test with high specificity (ability to correctly identify those without the condition) and a positive result effectively confirms the presence of the condition.
    				   - In other words, if the test accurately detects the presence of the condition when it's truly there, it's considered helpful for confirming the condition.
    				   - In the context of the SLR test, "SpPin" suggests that a cross-positive SLR (reproduction of contralateral leg pain) is specific (unlikely to be positive in the absence of the condition) and helpful for confirming lumbar HNP with radiculopathy when the test result is positive.

				- A herniated disc typically causes radiating pain into the leg due to nerve root compression, which is not mentioned.[3][4][5]
				- Herniated discs often have neurological symptoms like numbness/tingling or weakness in the leg, which are not described.[4][5]

				- The acute traumatic lifting event is more consistent with a strain/sprain than a disc herniation, which can have a more insidious onset.[3][4]

.1.21. The Treatment-Based Classification (TBC) System
	-This system evolved as a response to the failures of the patho-anatomical model.
	-Treatment-Based Classification (TBC) is a model whereby the clinician makes treatment decisions based on the patient's clinical presentation.
	-The primary purpose of the TBC approach is to identify baseline patient characteristics that predict responsiveness to varying treatment strategies. 
	-The TBC for the spine is not without its critics, however. Reasons for this include:
		Some patients are difficult to classify.
			The overall reliability of an early system is 75.5% (Henry et al 2012) – which is not bad, but not high either. 
		It may not attend enough to psychosocial factors.
		There is conflicting research on its effectiveness.

							Specific Exercise Classification (AKA: Directional Preferences)
								-"The basic premise advocated for treating patients in a specific-exercise classification is
								to use repeated end-range movernents in the direction that caused centralization.
								-----------------------------------------
								Definitions ('Centralization Vs Peripheralization.png') [MSK2_CVs.P] (also from [MSK2_Live01_Back::CentrVsPeriph])
									For pt. with lumbar radiculopaty
										-To see how symptoms change in response to particular movements
										-An increase in symptoms is not considered here; it does not affect these concepts!! 
									---------------------------------------------------
									Centralization:
										1.Pain, or paresthesia that moves centrally, towards the spine
											-A pain or paresthesia which was present is abolished
											-A symptom diminished or abolished during a specific movement (or position) and remains so for at least 30 seconds
											-Symptoms of tingling, numbness, or pain are traveling towards the origin of pain
												I.e.: If a pt. with paresthesia while we take them to a particular movement, and the symptoms move proximally or the symptoms of the leg go away alltogether.
											-Sometimes, when we are centralizing the symptoms, this also coincides with a local low back pain so. Taking away the symptoms from the leg is the most important at the moment though.
											-PTs use patient positioning, moving testing, and certain manual tests to assess and cause centralization. This is then a desireble fact.  
									Peripheralization:
										1.A pain, or paresthesia moves distally, away from the spine
											-A paresthesia or radiating pain is produced which was not previously present
											-A symptom present (or produced) increases in intensity and remains increased for at least 30 seconds after completion of the movement.
											-Symptoms of tingling, numbness, or pain are traveling away from the origin of pain.
											-Not a desirable effect; avoid it
												Educate the patient to avoid that movement that provke this 
								-------------------------------------------
								Examination Findings [MSK2_Live01_Back]
									Status improves (centralizes) during movement testing
										• Single test movements
											• Flexion
											• Extension
											• Anterior/Posterior pelvic rotation
										• Repeated/Sustained movements
										• Very acute patients possibly only with sustained positions of flexion/extension/pelvic positioning
											--------------------------------
											I.e.: ('Examination Findings Centr vs Periph')
												-In this case we see that for this patient, flexion peripheralizes the symptoms and extension centralize them.
												 -You will imitate this movements when testing the pt.

							-Patients in this classification category may benefit from common considerations for education.

									-Patients with disc injuries may be at increased risk of re-injury with flexion early in the morning. This is due to rehydration of the discs and other tissues during the night. (Why if they are hydratated? ¿?)
