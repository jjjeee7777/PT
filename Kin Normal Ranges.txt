	.Scapulohumeral Rhythm
		.Scapulohumeral rhythm is the movement relationship between the shoulder girdle and shoulder joint. After the first 30°, for every 2° of shoulder joint flexion or abduction, the shoulder girdle rotates upwardly 1°.
		.The first 30° of shoulder joint motion is pure shoulder joint motion. However, after that, for every 2° of shoulder flexion or abduction that occurs, the scapula must upwardly rotate 1°. This 2:1 ratio is known as scapulohumeral rhythm.

Patellofemoral Joint (knee)
	Open-Packed Position: full extension
	Close-Package Position: full flexion
	-----------------------------------------
	*From full extension to 90° flexion, the femur articulates with the medial and lateral facets of the patella.
	*Beyond 90° of flexion (from fully extended and going down. Achieving 0° is fisically impossible.), the patella slips off the trochlear groove of the femur and the lateral and medial facets of the patellar make contact on the femoral condyles.
	*At 135° (from 90°=femur & tibia perpendicular and keep flexing), the lateral femoral condyle protrudes anteriorly to such a point that a transverse rotation of the patellar occurs. This rotation allows the odd facet to articulate on the medial condyle.
	------------------------------------------
	Patellar Motion
		During flexion, the patella moves inferiorly
		During extension, the patella moves superiorly

Temporomandibular Joint - Opening and Closing the Mouth
	Movements of the Temporomandibular Joint
		*Convex (head of mandible) on Concave (mandibular fossa & articular disk)
		-Elevation AKA flexion (close mouth) (mouth closed [elevated and retruded]) → posterior glide & anterior roll.
		-Depression AKA extension (open mouth) (mouth widely open [mandible protruded and depressed]) → anterior glide & posterior roll.

Arthrokinematics of the following cervical joints
	Occiput on C1 (A/0 joint) Atlanto-Occipital or Atlantooccipital Joint (AO)
		Two joints act as one joint to create nodding (transverse axis) and slight sidebending (ant-post axis) movements.
			-Flexion: occipital condyles roll anterior and glide posterior (and superior) on C1 articular surface of lateral mass.
			-Sidebending (~5°) with slight contralateral rotation:
				SIDE BENDING RIGHT:
					Minimal motion in this plane (think joint play)
					R occiput travels MIA (medially inferior, anterior)
					L occiput travels LPS (lateral, posterior, superior)
	C1 on C2 (A/A joint) Atlanto-Axial or Atlantoaxial Joint
		Movement occurs at the one median and two lateral A/A joints simultaneously. The lateral joints support the head's weight via the atlas, while the medial joint guides rotation.
			Rotation:
				1. Dens does not move, but acts as pivot around which movement occurs
				2. Inferior facet of atlas glides opposite to direction of rotation
				3. Contralateral facet glides anteriorly
				4. Ipsilateral facet glides posteriorly  
					Example: if you turn your head to the right, the right inferior facet will glide backwards and the left inferior facet will glide forwards
				● Total range is ~45°
				● Limited by alar ligaments
			Flexion/Extension:
				1. Inferior facets of atlas are convex, while superior facets of axis are concave → roll and glide opposite
				2. Minimal flexion/extension (~10°) occurs at these joints. During flexion, the atlas' inferior facets roll anteriorly, glide posteriorly and the atlas' anterior arch glides inferiorly. 
				3. The opposite motions occur during extension. Inferior facets roll posteriorly and glide anteriorly. Anterior arch glides superiorly.

		-Intervertebral joints create the flexion/extension motion and are guided by uncovertebral joints. The intervertebral joints also create sidebending that is coupled with rotation in plane with the facet joints.

Ribs
	What are the motions that occur at each rib area?
		Ribs 1 & 2
			Minimal with quiet resp. Move upward & laterally.
		Ribs 3 – 6
			The axis of rotation approximates frontal plane. "Pump Handle" movement increasing anterior/posterior diameter of the thorax.
		Ribs 7-10
			The axis of rotation approximates sagittal plane. "Bucket Handle" movement increasing transverse diameter of thorax with increase in infrasternal angle.
		Ribs 11 & 12 
			It can move slightly in all directions. Depressed by quadratus lumborum to form a fixed point of action for diaphragm.
